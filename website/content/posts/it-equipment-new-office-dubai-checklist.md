**IT equipment for new office setups in Dubai should be decided in this order: the internet line and cabling, the network equipment, the laptops or desktops and monitors, security and backup, then the extras such as meeting rooms, printers and phones.** The common mistake is leaving IT to the last week. Cabling and the internet line depend on the fit-out and the building, and they take the longest. This checklist is written for the founder, office manager or finance lead who has a move-in date and needs to know what to order and when.

If you are choosing the laptops first, read our [business laptops buying guide](/guides/business-laptops-uae-buying-guide). If you want someone to plan the whole thing, see our [IT consultancy and office setup](/services/it-consultancy-office-setup) service.

## Before you order anything: five decisions

1. **How many people, and in which roles?** Count office staff, people who travel, and anyone who runs design, engineering or video software. This sets the laptop models.
2. **Laptops or desktops?** Laptops suit most teams because people move between desks and meeting rooms and work from home. Desktops make sense at reception, in call-centre rows and for fixed workstations.
3. **Cloud or on-site?** Decide whether email, files and sign-in will run on Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace. This decides whether you need a server at all.
4. **Where will the equipment live?** Find out where the internet line enters the unit and where the network cabinet or shelf will go. Ventilation and a lockable space matter.
5. **What is the move-in date, and what is the date the first person starts?** Work back from it. Order the long-lead items first.

## IT equipment for new office: the checklist

### 1. Internet line

- Ask the building which providers serve it and how long installation takes.
- Choose a business plan. Ask about a fixed IP address if you need remote access or a CCTV system.
- For offices where downtime is expensive, plan a second line from a different provider with automatic fail-over.

### 2. Structured cabling

- A network point at every desk, in meeting rooms, at reception and at the printer location.
- Cabling is done during the fit-out, before the walls and ceilings are closed. It is much more expensive to add later.
- Ceiling points for Wi-Fi access points, and a clear route to the network cabinet.

### 3. Network equipment

- A business firewall or router, not a home router.
- Switches with enough ports for every point plus spares, and PoE ports for access points, phones and cameras.
- Wi-Fi access points placed by coverage, not by guessing. A site survey is worth it for anything larger than a small unit. Our [networking and Wi-Fi](/services/networking-wifi) service covers this.
- A separate guest Wi-Fi network, so visitors never share the staff network.

### 4. Laptops, desktops and monitors

- Choose two or three standard models, not twelve. One for most staff, one light model for people who travel, a workstation for design or engineering.
- 16 GB of memory for most people, 32 GB for finance or heavy users. Avoid 8 GB machines.
- Decide keyboard layouts (Arabic/English or English-only) before ordering, because the layout is part of the part number.
- One or two monitors per desk, and a USB-C dock so each person connects with one cable.
- Ask for the laptops to be named, imaged or registered for Windows Autopilot, so they are ready on day one.

### 5. Accounts, sign-in and security

- Set up Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace with your own domain before day one, so every new starter gets an email address.
- Turn on multi-factor authentication for everyone from the start.
- Use Windows 11 Pro and BitLocker disk encryption, so a lost laptop does not mean lost data.
- Install endpoint protection and keep a short, written list of who has admin rights. See our [cybersecurity](/services/cybersecurity) service if you want help with this.

### 6. Backup

- Cloud files (OneDrive, SharePoint or Google Drive) as the main store, so work is not only on one laptop.
- A separate backup of important data, tested by restoring a file once.
- If you keep a local file server or NAS, back it up off-site too.

### 7. Printers and scanners

- One shared multifunction printer for most offices, on the network, with its own cable.
- Ask about the running cost per page, not just the price of the printer.

### 8. Meeting rooms

- A display or projector, a conferencing camera, a speaker and microphone suited to the room size, and a cable or wireless way to share a screen.
- Test with a real call before the first client visit. Our [AV and meeting room](/services/av-meeting-rooms) service covers the equipment and the installation.

### 9. Phones

- Most offices now use an IP phone system or softphones over the internet, with a UAE number routed to staff. See [IP telephony](/services/ip-telephony).

### 10. Physical security

- Access control for the entrance and sensitive rooms, and CCTV where needed. See [access control](/services/access-control) and [CCTV](/services/cctv-surveillance).
- A lockable cabinet for the network equipment and spare laptops.

### 11. After the move

- A support plan for the first year: who do staff call, and how fast do they get an answer. See [IT AMC and support](/services/it-amc-support).
- An asset register with serial numbers of every laptop, monitor and device. Ask your supplier to list serial numbers on the invoice.

## A suggested order of purchase

| When | What |
| --- | --- |
| As soon as the unit is confirmed | Internet line request; cabling plan; network equipment list |
| During the fit-out | Cabling installed; network cabinet position; access control and CCTV cabling |
| 3–4 weeks before move-in | Laptops, monitors and docks ordered; accounts and domain set up |
| 1–2 weeks before | Network installed and tested; laptops imaged and delivered; printer and meeting rooms installed |
| Move-in week | Walk through every desk and room; test every network point and Wi-Fi in each corner |
| After move-in | Backup test; asset register; support plan |

## Mistakes we see

- **Cabling forgotten during fit-out.** Fixing it later means opening ceilings and walls.
- **A home router and a consumer Wi-Fi extender.** It works for four people, and fails at fourteen.
- **Twelve laptop models.** Support gets harder with every extra model.
- **No decision on keyboards.** Arabic/English and English-only keyboards cannot be swapped later.
- **No backup test.** A backup that has never been restored is a hope, not a backup.
