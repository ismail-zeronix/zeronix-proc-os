## Business laptops vs consumer laptops

A consumer laptop from a mall can look the same on paper, but business lines (ThinkPad, EliteBook, ProBook, Latitude and Dell Pro, MacBook for some teams) differ where companies feel it:

- **Security built in:** a TPM 2.0 chip for BitLocker encryption, fingerprint readers, IR cameras for Windows Hello, and BIOS controls your IT team can lock.
- **Stable models:** the same model stays on sale for a year or more, so the 20th laptop matches the first one and one disk image fits all.
- **Windows 11 Pro:** needed to join a company domain or Microsoft Entra ID, and for BitLocker management.
- **Service:** business warranties, often with on-site options, and spare parts that stay available.

## The specs that matter (and the ones that don't)

| Part | What most office staff need | When to go higher |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Processor | Intel Core Ultra 5 (or AMD Ryzen 5 class) | Core Ultra 7 for heavy Excel models, many virtual meetings, light design |
| Memory | 16 GB | 32 GB for finance, analysts, developers, or laptops kept 4+ years |
| Storage | 512 GB SSD | 1 TB if files are kept locally rather than in OneDrive or SharePoint |
| Screen | 14-inch, 1920x1200, 300–400 nits | 500 nits for people who work near windows or outdoors |
| Weight | Around 1.3–1.4 kg | About 1 kg for people who travel every week |
| Security | TPM 2.0, fingerprint or IR camera | Discrete TPM and privacy shutter for legal, finance and HR |

Two common mistakes: buying **8 GB** machines (too little for Microsoft 365, Teams and a browser in 2026), and buying laptops with **soldered memory** for teams that may need more later. The ThinkPad T14 Gen 5 has two memory slots; the X1 Carbon's memory is soldered, so choose enough at purchase.

## UAE details to get right before you order

**Warranty.** Ask for the warranty of the exact part number: its length, whether it is carry-in or on-site, and whether it is valid outside the UAE. Middle East models are sometimes only serviced in countries where that model is sold, which matters for staff who travel.

**Keyboards.** Decide how many Arabic/English and English-only keyboards you need before ordering; the keyboard is part of the part number, not something to swap later.

**VAT and paperwork.** You should get a UAE tax invoice with 5% VAT and your TRN, plus serial numbers for your asset register.

**Delivery and set-up.** For more than a handful of machines, ask for them imaged and named before delivery, or for Windows Autopilot registration so they set themselves up when staff sign in.

## Mac or Windows for a company?

Windows business laptops are the default for most UAE companies because they join Microsoft Entra ID or Active Directory, run desktop software such as Tally, Sage and many ERP clients, and are managed with Intune and BitLocker. MacBooks suit teams whose tools all run in the browser (CRMs, Microsoft 365 on the web, Google Workspace, design and marketing apps) and who value battery life. Many companies mix the two: Windows for finance and operations, Mac for marketing and management. If you do, make sure your IT provider manages both.

## How many models should a company standardise on?

Two or three. One standard laptop for most staff, one light model for people who travel, and a workstation for anyone who runs CAD, BIM or video. Fewer models means one disk image, shared chargers and docks, and spare parts that fit everyone.

## Buying for a team: a short checklist

1. Count users by role (office, travelling, design) and the keyboard layout each needs.
2. Choose memory for the life of the laptop: 16 GB for three years, 32 GB for four or more.
3. Ask for the warranty of the exact part number, and whether you need on-site service.
4. Decide on docks and monitors at the same time; USB-C and Thunderbolt docks work across most current business laptops.
5. Ask for a quotation with delivery time, serial numbers on the invoice, and imaging or Autopilot if you need it.
