**The right laptops for schools in the UAE are standard business laptops for teachers and staff, lower-cost models or desktops for labs and student use, all on one standard image, with Windows 11 Pro, 16 GB of memory and a business warranty.** The most common mistake is buying the cheapest consumer laptops with 8 GB of memory: they are slow within a year and hard to manage across a school.

## Three groups, three kinds of laptop

Schools do not need one laptop. They need three kinds, each with a different job and budget.

| Group | What they do | What to buy |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Teachers and staff | Lessons, marking, reports, video calls, admin work | Business laptop, 14-inch, 16 GB, 512 GB SSD, Windows 11 Pro |
| Computer lab and shared use | Taught lessons, exams, fixed benches | Desktops, or low-cost business laptops, one standard image |
| Student devices, if provided | Learning platforms, writing, browsing | Durable, low-cost laptops with a manageable image |
| Creative and media departments | Video, design, music | Higher-memory laptops or Macs, as the software needs |

Treat each group separately in the quotation, with its own quantity and its own model, so one cheap model does not end up on the head teacher's desk and one expensive model does not end up in 200 student bags.

## Laptops for schools: the specs that matter

- **Memory:** 16 GB for teachers and staff; avoid 8 GB for anyone, as learning platforms and browsers use more every year. Lab machines can be 8 GB only if they run a very light, managed image.
- **Storage:** 512 GB for teachers. Smaller SSDs are fine for students whose work is in the cloud.
- **Processor:** a current Intel Core Ultra 5 or similar is enough for most staff.
- **Battery:** teachers move between classrooms. Look for a laptop that lasts a school day on a charge.
- **Build:** students and teachers carry laptops in bags. Business lines are sturdier than entry consumer models, and some are tested to a durability standard. Check what the part number actually lists.
- **Security:** a TPM 2.0 chip for disk encryption protects student and staff data if a laptop is lost.
- **Operating system:** Windows 11 Pro for domain or Entra ID join and BitLocker. Education versions or Chromebooks have their own management, so choose one system for the school and stay with it.

## Managing a fleet of school laptops

A school with 300 laptops does not manage 300 laptops. It manages one standard image and a list.

1. **One image.** Set up one laptop with the software, settings and security you want, and copy it to the rest. Or register the laptops for Windows Autopilot so they set themselves up when the teacher signs in.
2. **Device management.** Use Microsoft Intune or a similar tool to update, lock down and wipe laptops remotely.
3. **An asset register.** Record the serial number of every laptop, who has it and where it is. Ask your supplier to list serial numbers on the invoice.
4. **Labels.** Label each laptop with an asset number so it can be found.
5. **Spares.** Keep a few spare laptops so a broken one is replaced the same day.

## Charging and storage

If laptops stay at school, plan charging. A charging trolley or cabinet holds, charges and locks a class set, and it saves lessons that start with finding chargers. Check that the trolley fits the laptop model you choose, and that the room has enough power points.

## Warranty, accidents and repairs

Schools break laptops: screens crack, keys come off, batteries wear. Ask for:

- the warranty length and type of the exact part number (see our [laptop warranty guide](/guides/laptop-warranty-uae-middle-east-vs-international));
- whether accidental damage cover is available, and what it costs;
- a spare stock plan with your supplier, so repairs do not leave a class without devices;
- a clear contact for repairs.

## Ordering for the start of term

1. Count teachers, labs, students and spares by school.
2. Decide the standard model for each group and the memory it needs.
3. Choose the keyboard layouts and the warranty.
4. Ask for imaging or Autopilot registration if you do not have an IT team to do it.
5. Order well before the term starts, and ask for staged delivery so each school gets its machines on time.
6. Check any requirements from your school group or regulator before you order.
