**A Middle East laptop warranty is the manufacturer's warranty for a laptop sold in the region, and it is normally valid and serviced in the countries where that same model is sold, which is not always the same as worldwide.** An international warranty is wider, but is not available on every model. The right question is never "does it have a warranty?" but "what is the warranty on this exact part number, and where does it work?"

## What a Middle East warranty usually means

Manufacturers sell the same laptop family through different regions, and each regional channel has its own part numbers, its own keyboard options and its own warranty terms. A laptop sold in the Middle East is normally supported by the manufacturer's service network in the Middle East.

In practice that means:

- The warranty period and type are set per part number, and are shown on the quotation or the manufacturer's page for that part number.
- Service is arranged through the manufacturer's authorised partners.
- The warranty may be valid only in some countries, or only where that same model is officially sold.

Some brands also offer international options, sometimes as a paid upgrade. We do not assume one for any model: we state what applies to the part number we quote.

## Why this matters for companies

Most of the time, none of this matters. A laptop fails in Dubai, the supplier tells you where to take it, and it is repaired. It matters in three situations:

1. **Staff who travel.** A sales manager on a two-week trip whose laptop fails abroad may find that the local service centre cannot service a model that is not sold in that country.
2. **Staff who relocate.** A laptop moved to an office in another country may no longer be covered.
3. **Grey imports.** A cheap laptop from an unofficial source may have a warranty that is valid only in its original country. Ask who stands behind the warranty in the UAE.

## The five questions to ask about the exact part number

1. **How long is the warranty?** Years for the laptop, in writing.
2. **Is it carry-in or on-site?** On-site means an engineer comes to you.
3. **Is the battery covered, and for how long?** Often shorter than the laptop.
4. **Where is it valid?** The UAE only, the Middle East, or worldwide.
5. **Who do I contact?** Your supplier, the manufacturer or a service partner, and the route for a claim.

## Laptop warranty: carry-in or on-site, which one do you need?

| | Carry-in | On-site |
| --- | --- | --- |
| How it works | You take it to a service centre, or arrange pickup | An engineer comes to your office |
| Typical use | Standard office laptops | Workstations, executives, teams that cannot be without a machine |
| Time without the laptop | Days, depending on the repair | Often a short visit, depending on the contract |
| Cost | Usually included in the standard warranty | Often an extra pack at purchase |

A common approach for companies: carry-in for most staff, with a few spare laptops in the office to lend out, and on-site for the people who cannot wait.

## Extended warranty: when it is worth it

Extended cover is the one part of a laptop warranty decision that has a deadline, so it is worth thinking about before the order, not after the delivery.

- **Worth it** for laptops you keep four years or more, for workstations, and for machines in the field or travelling often.
- **Optional** for laptops you replace every three years and have spares for.
- **Decide at purchase.** Most brands sell extended packs only at the time of purchase or shortly after, so decide when you order.

## What a warranty does not cover

It helps to know the limits before you need to make a claim.

A manufacturer's warranty covers faults in the hardware under normal use. It does not usually cover accidental damage such as spills and drops, loss or theft, software problems caused by malware, or battery wear beyond what the warranty states. Some brands sell accidental damage cover as an extra. If your team works in the field, ask for it.

## A simple rule for company orders

Write the warranty into the purchase order, not just the quotation. List the part number, the warranty length and type, the battery period and the countries where it is valid. If the laptop that arrives does not match, you have a document to point to. Keep a copy with the asset register, next to the serial numbers.

## If something goes wrong

1. Note the serial number and what happened.
2. Tell your supplier. They will tell you whether it is a warranty case and where it should be serviced.
3. Back up your data first where you can. Repairs can include a new drive or a reset.
4. Keep the invoice. The serial number and the invoice date are what start a warranty claim.
