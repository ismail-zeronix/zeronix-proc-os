**A good corporate laptop supplier in Dubai quotes the exact part number, states the warranty in writing, issues a UAE tax invoice with your TRN, keeps the same model in stock for later orders, and is still there after the delivery.** Twelve questions to ask any supplier, ours included, are below. If you get clear answers to all twelve, you are dealing with a supplier. If you get vague ones, you are dealing with a shop.

This guide is for the person who signs off the purchase: an office manager, finance lead, procurement officer or IT manager buying 5, 20 or 200 laptops. ## Why your corporate laptop supplier matters as much as the laptop

Two suppliers can quote the same ThinkPad at a similar price and give you very different experiences. The laptop is the same. What changes is whether the part number matches the keyboard you need, whether the warranty is what you thought, whether the invoice is accepted by your accountant, whether the second batch matches the first, and who answers when one laptop has a faulty battery in month eight.

That is why the questions below are about the supplier's process, not the machine.

## The 12 questions

### 1. Which exact part number are you quoting?

Business laptops share a name but come in many part numbers with different memory, storage, keyboard, processor and warranty. "ThinkPad T14" or "EliteBook 840" is not a quotation. A supplier who quotes the full part number, or the full specification, is telling you exactly what will arrive. This is also the only way to compare two quotations fairly.

### 2. Is it a Middle East part number, and what warranty comes with it?

Ask for the warranty length, whether it is carry-in or on-site, whether the battery is covered for the same period, and whether the warranty works outside the UAE. The answer for the exact part number is what counts, not the brand's general policy. Our page on [delivery and warranty](/delivery-warranty) explains what we state on every quotation, and our guide to [Middle East versus international laptop warranty](/guides/laptop-warranty-uae-middle-east-vs-international) goes into the detail.

### 3. Which keyboard layouts are available?

Tell the supplier how many Arabic/English and English-only keyboards you need. The layout is part of the part number, so it cannot be fixed after delivery without replacing the keyboard. See [Arabic/English keyboards on business laptops](/guides/arabic-english-keyboard-laptops-uae) for how to check this.

### 4. Is the stock in the UAE, and for how long is it reserved?

"Available" can mean on the shelf, at the distributor, or ordered from abroad. Ask where the stock is today, how long the price and the stock are held on your quotation, and what happens if the price moves before you order.

### 5. Will I receive a UAE tax invoice with my TRN?

A company needs an invoice with the supplier's TRN and its own, showing 5% VAT. Ask for a sample if you have not bought from them before. Your accountant will need it for the VAT return, and it also matters when a purchase is later audited.

### 6. Are serial numbers listed on the invoice or delivery note?

Serial numbers are what your IT team uses for the asset register, for warranty claims and for insurance. A supplier who lists them as a matter of course is one who expects you to come back with a claim.

### 7. Can you deliver the laptops set up?

Ask whether they can name the machines, apply your standard image, or register them for Windows Autopilot. For anything beyond about ten laptops, this saves your IT person days of work. Ask what it costs and how much time it adds. If the supplier cannot do it, ask who they work with.

### 8. How do you deliver, and can you deliver in stages?

Delivery across the Emirates should be on the quotation with a date, not "within a few days". For a larger order, staged delivery lets people start working while the rest is prepared.

### 9. What happens when a laptop fails?

The honest answer is "we tell you whether it is a warranty case, where it is serviced, and how long it usually takes". The bad answer is "contact the manufacturer". A supplier that is involved after the sale will take the first call, give you the service route and follow up.

### 10. Can I get the same model again in six months?

Companies grow. The twentieth laptop should match the first so that one disk image, one dock and one spare charger fit everyone. Ask whether the supplier can supply the same part number later and whether they will tell you before a model is replaced.

### 11. Who is my contact?

A name, a phone number and an email, not a general inbox. For a company account, you should be able to message the same person for the second order.

### 12. What are the payment terms?

Ask about the payment method, whether a deposit is needed, and whether a company with a trade licence and a history can have credit terms. Do not pay in full in advance to a supplier you have not checked.

## Red flags

- A price with no part number or specification.
- "Warranty as per the manufacturer" with no length or type.
- No TRN, or an invoice that does not name your company.
- A very low price on a model that is not on the manufacturer's current business range.
- Stock that is "arriving soon" with no date.
- No physical address, no trade licence number, no named contact.
