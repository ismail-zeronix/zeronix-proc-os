## What the UAE data protection law means for laptops

The UAE Personal Data Protection Law, Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021, is modelled on Europe's GDPR. It does not name any hardware, but it expects organisations that handle personal data to take appropriate technical and organisational measures against unauthorised access, loss or disclosure. Firms in free zones such as DIFC and ADGM also have their own data protection regimes.

For laptops, "appropriate measures" in practice means:

1. **Full-disk encryption** (BitLocker on Windows 11 Pro, FileVault on Mac), so a lost device does not expose data.
2. **Strong sign-in** with fingerprint or face recognition, so people do not choose weak passwords to save time.
3. **Managed devices**: laptops joined to Microsoft Entra ID or the firm's domain, so IT can enforce policies and wipe a lost device.
4. **Physical privacy**: camera shutters and privacy screens for work in public places.

This is general information, not legal advice; your firm's compliance lead should set the policy.

## Specs for document-heavy legal work

Legal work is documents, not graphics: long PDFs, contract comparisons, e-discovery exports, Microsoft Word with tracked changes, and video hearings. That calls for 16 GB of memory (32 GB for litigation teams handling large bundles), a fast 512 GB or 1 TB SSD, a sharp 14-inch screen at 1920x1200 or better, and a dock with a second monitor so a lawyer can draft on one screen and read on the other. A good keyboard matters more than processor speed for people who type all day.

## Arabic and English work

UAE firms work in both languages: court filings, translations and client correspondence. Order Arabic/English keyboards for staff who type Arabic, check that your document management and e-signature tools handle right-to-left text, and install Arabic proofing tools in Microsoft 365. The keyboard is part of the part number and cannot be changed after delivery, so decide per person before ordering.

## Common mistakes

- **Consumer laptops with Windows Home:** no BitLocker management and no joining the firm's directory.
- **Shared logins for trainees or secretaries:** every person needs their own sign-in for an audit trail.
- **Encryption without recovery keys in the directory:** a forgotten PIN then locks out the files for good.

## Before the laptops reach the lawyers

- Turn on BitLocker and store the recovery keys in Microsoft Entra ID or your directory, not on paper.
- Enrol fingerprints or faces on day one.
- Register the laptops in your device management (for example Intune) so a lost one can be wiped.
- Record serial numbers against each person in the asset register.
- Give partners and anyone who travels a privacy filter.
