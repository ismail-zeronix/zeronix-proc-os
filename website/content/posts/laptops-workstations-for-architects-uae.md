## What Revit, AutoCAD and rendering actually use

- **Processor:** Revit and AutoCAD do much of their work on one core, so **high clock speed** matters more than core count. Rendering and exporting use all cores.
- **Memory:** Revit loads the whole model into memory. **32 GB** is the working minimum; **64 GB or more** for large, linked or federated models.
- **Graphics:** a professional **NVIDIA RTX** GPU keeps 3D views smooth and runs real-time renderers. Enscape, Twinmotion and Lumion want **8 GB or more** of GPU memory for full projects.
- **Storage:** a fast NVMe SSD (1 TB or more) shortens opening and saving; keep central models on a server or a cloud workspace.
- **Screen:** 16-inch at 1920x1200 or better for laptops; for desks, two 27-inch monitors.

## Laptop or tower: choosing workstations for architects

| Need | Choose |
| --- | --- |
| Present to clients, work on site, hot-desk | Mobile workstation |
| Large BIM models, frequent rendering, visualisation | Tower workstation |
| AutoCAD 2D and light 3D | Light mobile workstation |
| Both | A tower at the desk and a light mobile workstation, or a mobile workstation with a dock and two monitors |

A tower gives more memory, a bigger GPU and better cooling for the same budget. Many studios give visualisation and BIM-coordination staff towers, and project architects a mobile workstation with a dock.

## Monitors for architecture work

Screen space saves more time than most upgrades. Two 27-inch monitors at 2560x1440 give room for plans, 3D views and the properties panel side by side; visualisers who judge colour need monitors that cover most of the sRGB or DCI-P3 range and can be calibrated. Connect them through a Thunderbolt dock so the architect plugs in one cable at the desk. Check that the laptop can drive the number of screens you want: the P16s Gen 3 supports up to three external monitors.

## Common mistakes studios make

- **Gaming laptops instead of workstations.** They can be fast, but they lack ISV certification, business warranty and quiet cooling for long renders.
- **16 GB of memory.** Revit with linked models fills it quickly; 32 GB is the working minimum.
- **Buying the GPU by price alone.** Real-time rendering needs GPU memory; 4 GB cards stall on full projects.
- **Ignoring heat and power.** Towers need ventilation and a UPS; a power cut in the middle of a sync can damage a central model.
- **Keeping central models on a laptop.** Put central files on a server or cloud workspace and back them up daily.

## A sensible set-up for a 10-person studio

- **Project architects (6):** mobile workstations with 32 GB of RAM and an 8 GB RTX GPU, a Thunderbolt dock and two 27-inch monitors at the desk.
- **BIM coordinator and visualiser (2):** tower workstations with 64–128 GB of RAM and a stronger RTX GPU.
- **Admin and accounts (2):** standard business laptops such as the [ThinkPad T14 Gen 5](/products/laptops/lenovo-thinkpad-t14-gen-5).
- **Shared:** a file server or cloud workspace for central models, a UPS for the towers, and a large-format plotter.

## UAE buying notes

- Ask for the warranty of the exact part number; workstations are worth on-site service, so a failure does not stop a submission.
- Check Autodesk's certified hardware list for the GPU and driver you will use.
- Workstations in the UAE heat need ventilated desks and a UPS; dust filters help in towers.
