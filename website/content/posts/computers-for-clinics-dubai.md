## NABIDH, your EMR, and what it means for hardware

Dubai Health Authority requires licensed facilities to connect their electronic medical records to **NABIDH**, Dubai's health information exchange. DHA does not specify computer models; your **EMR vendor** decides which operating systems, browsers and devices are supported, and must meet NABIDH's security requirements.

So start with three questions to the EMR vendor:

1. Which Windows version and browser do you support, and is it web-based or installed?
2. Which devices do you integrate with (card readers, Emirates ID readers, label and receipt printers, scanners)?
3. Do any devices need older ports (serial or VGA) or special drivers?

Clinics licensed in Abu Dhabi (DoH) or the northern Emirates (MOHAP) have their own systems; the same questions apply.

## Monitors, printers and the front-desk kit

The computer is only part of a clinic desk. Reception usually needs a 24-inch monitor, a receipt or invoice printer, a label printer for samples, an Emirates ID card reader, a card payment terminal and a scanner for insurance documents. Doctors' rooms need a 24-inch monitor, sometimes a second one for results, and a small printer for prescriptions if your EMR does not send them electronically. Before buying, ask your EMR and insurance-portal vendors which printers and readers they support, and keep a spare of each small device so a failure does not stop the front desk.

## Network and Wi-Fi

A reliable network matters more than fast computers: the EMR, NABIDH, insurance approvals and card payments all stop when it does. Use wired connections for fixed desks where you can, a separate guest Wi-Fi for patients, and a UPS for the router and switches. Ask your IT provider for a business-grade router and firewall rather than the default ISP box.

## Common mistakes

- **Shared reception logins:** every receptionist needs their own sign-in so actions can be traced.
- **Consumer PCs:** they lack TPM-based encryption management and business warranty.
- **No spare devices:** one broken label printer can stop sample collection.

## Protecting patient data on clinic computers

- One sign-in per person, never a shared "reception" account.
- Disk encryption on every computer (the TPM 2.0 chip makes this simple on Windows 11 Pro).
- Screens that lock automatically after a short idle time, and privacy filters at reception.
- Windows, browser and EMR updates on a schedule.
- A UPS at reception and for the server or network rack.
